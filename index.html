Who We Are

Healthcare
reimagined

Duke Business Behind Health (DBBH) is Duke's first undergraduate student-led initiative dedicated to reshaping how students understand and engage with the health economy.

So many students come into Duke thinking pre-med is the only way to make an impact in health. DBBH is a space for students interested in health to learn, explore, and find their career, as well as build a community of like-minded peers and mentors along the way. We want to show you the diversity in health and empower you to build solutions in or out of a traditional career path.

We're reimagining healthcare not just as a field of medicine, but as a space for builders, thinkers, creators, and innovators from all disciplines.

IncubatorApplication-Based
Current program · 2026–2027

A year-long program that matches Duke undergraduates with real-world health innovation projects, supported by experienced mentors from academia, industry, and nonprofits. Interdisciplinary teams of 5–6 work across three tracks: Data Science, Consulting, and Technology (biotech and devices), running from project brief through a showcase where fellows present their work to a live audience and guest judges.

Last year · Fall 2025

The inaugural Fall 2025 cohort ran as a 10–12 week program, putting interdisciplinary teams of 5–6 on 7 curated, mentor-supported projects across biotech, healthcare delivery, and health equity, running from project brief to the November Incubator Showcase, where Fellows presented their work to a live audience and guest judges, then stuck around for the DBBH Social.

CorporateApplication-Based

Not a one-off panel but a professional development ecosystem. Corporate gives students repeated, hands-on exposure to the people, pathways, and skills shaping healthcare: candid career conversations with leaders across medicine and industry, recruiting insights from students headed to major health companies, practical workshops with the Duke Career Center, and immersive looks inside fields like sports medicine.

Recent corporate programming
  • Beyond the White Coat: 75+ students. Two leaders shaping healthcare from completely different angles. Duke medtech founder Dr. Bruce Klitzman and UNC Health Chief Medical Informatics Officer Dr. David McSwain offered a candid look at the careers in the intersection of business and health nobody tells you about early enough.
  • How to Land Your Internship: 80+ attendees and a panel of five upperclassmen headed to Goldman Sachs, McKinsey, Merck, Medtronic, Mount Sinai Medical School, and ClearView Healthcare Partners broke down how they turned STEM backgrounds into careers, sharing recruiting strategies and the paths that got them there.
  • Resume Workshop with the Duke Career Center: a hands-on session led by Assistant Directors Michael Rudisill and Grace Athy, followed by small-group breakouts to dissect successful resumes across consulting, life sciences, biotech, engineering, healthcare, and research, then apply those insights to students' own resumes.
  • Play Smart: Inside Sports Medicine: an inside look at how elite athletes get back in the game with Carolina Hurricanes physical therapist Nick Mahaffey and Duke Athletics orthopedic surgeon Dr. Brian Lau, featuring a live knee-wrapping demo and review of famous basketball injury footage to unpack how professionals diagnose, treat, and prevent injuries at the highest level.
  • Coffee Chat series: 1–2 times per month, 4–5 students sit down with an industry leader. Our most recent highlight: Meg Powell, Managing Director of 501 Ventures, founder of Target RWE, and board director at Blue Cross NC.

About dBBH
Duke's first undergraduate student-led initiative dedicated to reshaping how students understand and engage with the health economy.

DBBH was founded after noticing a gap at Duke: there were many clubs focused on business and many clubs focused on pre-health, but none focused on the intersection of the two, or the breadth of careers inside the healthcare economy. Two years later, that gap became a community: 300+ members, over 500 people brought together through two conferences, multiple professional development programs, a podcast, and an industry network that keeps growing.

– Gabrielle Perry and Ella Etchandy
